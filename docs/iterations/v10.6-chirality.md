A reflection about the torso axis maps the hip midpoint and the shoulder midpoint to themselves, so
10.5's fit is blind to it by construction. Chirality has to come from somewhere else, explicitly.

Two independent mechanisms, and both are needed:

1. **Anatomy** — `manifest.mirrored` against the patient's own live camera facing. This decides
   whether left and right labels need swapping.
2. **Geometry** — a facing discriminator measured on *both* bodies, deciding whether the horizontal
   axis needs negating. Frontal: the sign of labelled-right minus labelled-left shoulder. Sagittal:
   the sign of nose minus hip midpoint, because the shoulder line collapses to nearly a point there.

Below a margin on either, draw nothing.

Tests:

- **Mirror sensitivity** — feed a reflected copy and assert the pipeline **disagrees**. A ghost that
  passes both is broken. This is the test that would have caught the §6.16 arrow bug.
- **Asymmetry probe** — the *extended* limb must become the other limb.
- Undecidable facing draws nothing.

Device gate: record on the rear camera and play it back on the front, then the reverse. The ghost's
moving limb must match the patient's moving limb in all four combinations.

**Finding: the "geometric assertion against the stored bit" in this spec cannot exist.** A mirror
body is a valid body. 10.1 already proves `normalisedPose(reflected + relabelled) == mirrored()`,
and a person facing either camera shows their *labelled*-right shoulder on screen-left, because the
front camera flips both the image and the labels. So the facing measure (mechanism 2) reads the same
from both cameras and says nothing about mechanism 1. No per-frame quantity distinguishes a body
from its mirror image. The stored `manifest.mirrored` plus the live camera facing are therefore the
**only** source of chirality, and the cross-camera device gate — not code — is what verifies them.
The first iteration's notes flagged exactly this family of tautology in its mirror test; this is
the same fact from the other side. A test pins it so that a real geometric check, if one is ever
found, has to be added deliberately.

Margin tuning (`GHOST_FACING_MARGIN`) still needs real recordings, and is left for C3 with the other
uncalibrated constants.

## Background worth knowing before starting

`PoseLandmarkerSource` flips the bitmap (`postScale(-1f, 1f)`) for the front camera **before**
inference, so MediaPipe labels a front-camera subject's anatomical right shoulder `LEFT_SHOULDER`.
`core/pose/Canonicalisation.kt` was written to correct exactly this and **is never called anywhere
in `app/src/main`** — the tree already carries a dead second mirroring convention. Do not route the
ghost through it and do not create a third; either delete it in its own change or leave it be.

`manifest.mirrored` is already stored in every bundle, so the anatomy bit is available rather than
inferred. Use the stored bit as the source of truth and the geometric discriminator as an assertion
against it — if they contradict, draw nothing.

## Traps

- **Normalise both sides by their own torso length before comparing.** The reference is in torso
  lengths and the patient is in fractions of the frame; the raw numbers differ by whatever the
  patient's distance from the camera happens to be, and one margin cannot judge both.
- **The test asymmetry must be in the projected plane.** The synthetic generator's knee and elbow
  both sweep in y and z, so a *frontal* projection of that movement is symmetric whichever way round
  it is — a mirror test built on it passes while mirrored and certifies the bug. Build a fixture
  with an asymmetry the projection can actually see, such as one arm held out sideways.
- **An arm held out sideways does not change sides when mirrored** — it stays on its own side and
  only reaches further. The assertion is about which limb is the reaching one, not which half of the
  screen a wrist lands in.
- A mirrored *leading* ghost is worse than a mirrored static one: the patient actively copies it.
  When in doubt, draw nothing.
