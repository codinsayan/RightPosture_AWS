# Version 11.3 — Watch-list size for compound exercises

Status: in-progress — implemented and unit tested; device gate not yet run.  
Source: §6.10, §6.17.1

Scope: raise `WATCH_LIST_SIZE` from 5 so an exercise built out of several joints gets more of them
into the frame score.

`frameScore` only covers watch-listed features, and the dominant one's z is ~0 by construction
because phase is derived from it. A compound exercise capped at five is therefore scored on four
joints at most, and loses ones it is genuinely being performed with.

§6.10's four PASS filters — ROM, tightness, visibility, salience — decide what is *eligible*; this
constant only caps how many survivors are kept. A single-joint exercise has nothing like this many
features clearing the filters, so it is unaffected.

Tests: generalise the two size tests in `WatchListTest` against the constant rather than spelling out
"five" — a test that names the number asserts the constant's current value rather than the behaviour
it controls.

Device gate: **this one is about what did *not* change.** Record a set before and after and compare
the per-rep and set quality numbers. Weights are salience over the sum of saliences, so admitting
more features redistributes them across everything.

## Why the gate is a comparison rather than a demo

The frame-score scale is load-bearing well beyond the score itself:

- `qualityBand`'s literal 55.0 is exactly the 2σ calibration point, and 80 is
  `PROGRESSION_MIN_QUALITY`.
- §6.18's CUSUM thresholds are expressed in quality points, so a shifted scale silently re-tunes
  fatigue detection.
- `HoldMode` carries a parallel, unweighted copy of the frame score that does not go through the
  watch list at all.

The list is ranked by salience, so the features this admits are by construction the least salient
ones — they dilute the joints that matter in proportion to how little they matter. That is the
argument for expecting the scale to hold. Check it rather than trusting it.

## Traps

- Re-check `VALIDATOR_MIN_WATCH_LIST_SIZE` against the new value.
- Do not fold a landmark-space distance term into `frameScore` as an alternative. It reaches joints
  no `FeatureId` captures, but it moves every number in the app relative to its sigma meaning and
  needs all three of the above re-derived. This constant gets most of the benefit for none of that.

## Result

- `core/Params.kt` — `WATCH_LIST_SIZE` 5 → 7: the dominant joint plus one per remaining major
  segment a whole-body movement recruits (hip, knee, ankle, trunk, shoulder, elbow). Comment
  records why and that the scale-holding argument is unverified.
- `VALIDATOR_MIN_WATCH_LIST_SIZE` re-checked: 3, still under the cap, and now pinned by a test.
- `WatchListTest` — the two size tests rewritten against the constant with a `rankedCandidates(n)`
  fixture, so they would have survived this change and will survive the next one. New
  `theSizeCapStillLeavesTheValidatorsFloorReachable`.

**It only affects exercises authored after it.** The watch list is chosen at authoring and stored
in the `.rpose`; a bundle already on a phone keeps its five features. That is also what makes the
device gate cheap: author the same exercise again and compare against the old bundle.

It also widens what 11.1 and 11.2 check. `poseConformant` looks at every watch-listed joint except
the dominant one, so two more joints can now make a frame non-conformant. They are the least
salient, so their σ is wide relative to their movement — but that interaction is a second reason
the gate needs a real set rather than a unit test.
