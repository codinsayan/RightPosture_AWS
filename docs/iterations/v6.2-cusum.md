## Deviation from the plan

**§6.18.5's SPARC cutoff is implemented as the *last* frequency above the threshold, not the
first crossing below it.** The plan says "truncate at the frequency where magnitude first falls
below 0.05". Taken literally that defeats the metric, and this was measured rather than assumed:

| profile | first below 0.05 | last above 0.05 |
|---|---|---|
| smooth half-sine | bin 3 (0.75 Hz) | bin 2 (0.50 Hz) |
| same plus a 9-cycle tremor | **bin 2 (0.50 Hz)** | bin 9 (2.25 Hz) |

A movement spectrum is not monotonic. The tremor sits in its own lobe at bin 9, but the spectrum
dips below the threshold at bin 2 on the way there, so the literal band stops *short of the
roughness* — and the rougher movement scores as the smoother one, which is backwards from the
plan's own statement that "a decrease indicates roughening movement". The published definition
(Balasubramanian et al.) takes the last frequency still above the threshold, and that is what makes
§6.18.5's claim true. `FatigueTest` pins it: a tremulous profile must score below a smooth one.

**SPARC is computed on the *speed* profile, not signed velocity.** SPARC normalises by the DC term,
and a rep's signed velocity is positive going out and negative coming back, so its DC term is
approximately zero and the normalisation divides by nothing. The literature applies SPARC to speed
for exactly this reason.

## Notes

- **The change point is the rep the decline began accumulating at, not the rep that alarmed.** On
  the safety-intervention fixture the alarm trips at rep 6, but rep 5 scored 88 against a baseline
  of 90, which is where the sum first leaves zero — so the answer is 5. This is what makes
  "deteriorated since rep 5" defensible instead of a vibe.
- **Both signals are reported separately, not just their conjunction.** §6.18.4 requires CUSUM *and*
  slope, and a reader who cannot see which of the two failed cannot tell a stable set from a noisy
  one that nearly fired. `alarmRep` is populated even when the status is `STABLE`.
- **The assessment runs live, on each completed rep.** §6.18.6 is an *intervention* — "stop the set,
  say so aloud, log it" cannot be said after the set has ended.
- **The stop is announced once.** A message that repeats every frame is noise, and noise is how a
  patient learns to ignore the one message that matters.
- **The safety stop outranks the rep count in the dispatcher.** Speaking "eight" over an instruction
  to stop would read as encouragement to continue.
- **No medical-validity claim.** The spoken line says form has dropped well below where it started
  and to rest — which is a thing the app measured. It says nothing about injury or diagnosis.
