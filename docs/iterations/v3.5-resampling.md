# Version 3.5 — Phase and resampling

Status: complete  
Source: §6.8

Scope: feature-derived phase, monotonic halves, 101-bin interpolation, separate ROM.

Tests: tempo invariance, stalled phase, undershoot ROM.

Gate: 101 finite bins per valid feature.

## Result

- Added `core/reference/PhaseResampling.kt`: `phaseProfile`, `resampleRep`, `resampleUsableReps`,
  and `ResampledRep`.
- Placed in `core`, not in an authoring-only package. §6.8.1 calls consistency between the two
  pipelines the subtlest correctness issue in the system: if authoring indexed the reference by time
  within the rep while the live pipeline derived phase from the dominant joint's value, a patient
  moving at a different tempo — the entire point of the product — would be scored against the wrong
  part of the reference, in a tempo-dependent way. One definition, one file, both callers.
- Phase is defined from the **dominant feature's value**, per §6.8.2, and the tempo-invariance test
  is the one that earns this iteration: the same movement at 120 samples and at 31 samples must
  land on the same 101 bins.
- §6.8.3 monotonicity enforced per half-rep — cumulative maximum outbound, cumulative minimum on
  the return. A test injects wobble large enough to reverse the raw ratio and asserts `p` never goes
  backwards, because inside a half-rep a reversal is noise or tremor and must not be modelled as
  phase.
- §6.8.4 stall guard implemented: when consecutive phase values differ by less than 1e-6 the earlier
  sample is taken rather than interpolating across a near-zero gap. A test holds the extremum for a
  third of the rep, which is the end-range pause §6.8.4 describes, and asserts all 101 bins stay
  finite.
- **ROM is recorded separately from phase.** §6.8.4 is explicit that these are different jobs.
  Phase normalises against the rep's own extremum, so a rep that falls short still spans 0 to 100
  and would otherwise hide its own undershoot. A test runs a full-range and a short rep through and
  asserts identical phase coverage but ROM of 80° versus 44°.
- A feature carrying a non-finite sample anywhere inside the rep is omitted from that rep rather
  than poisoning it, so the gate holds for everything returned.
- The mid-rep extremum is taken from the segmentation's alternating sequence rather than recomputed,
  so the two cannot disagree about where a rep turns around.

### Correction to the v3.4 note

v3.4 deferred §6.7.6 step 3 (shape outliers) to "3.5". That was wrong and is corrected in both
`v3.4-segmentation.md` and the changelog: resampling alone is not enough. The step thresholds RMS
deviation against a provisional **median trajectory** using **MAD**, and both arrive in 3.6.
Writing a one-off median and MAD here would duplicate what 3.6 builds properly — the same
duplication the original deferral existed to avoid.
