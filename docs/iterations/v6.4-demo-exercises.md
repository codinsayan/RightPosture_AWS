# Version 6.4 — Four demo exercises

Status: in-progress (device gate outstanding)
Source: §3.3, §8, §17

Scope: manifests and faults for knee extension, hip abduction, wall sit, sit-to-stand.

Tests: each exercise authors, validates, scores, cues, and finishes.

Device gate: all four pass safely with performer, stand, fitted clothing, and clear space.

---

## What was built

`catalog/DemoExercises.kt` — §3.3's four exercises, and the check that a recording is of the one
that was chosen.

The catalogue is **descriptive, not prescriptive**. It supplies no thresholds and no tolerances:
every number the live pipeline scores against still comes from the physio's own recording, which is
the whole argument of §6.11. What it supplies is what a recording cannot contain — the name, the
scoring path, where the phone goes, and what it is unsafe to keep doing.

| Exercise | Mode | Required view | Expected dominant | Compensation watched |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seated knee extension | cyclic | sagittal | `knee_flex_active` | `trunk_flexion` |
| Standing hip abduction | cyclic | **frontal** | `hip_abduction_active` | `pelvic_obliquity`, `trunk_lateral` |
| Wall sit | **hold** | sagittal | `knee_flex_active` or `hip_flex_active` | `trunk_flexion` |
| Sit to stand | cyclic | sagittal | hip, knee or trunk | `trunk_flexion`, `ankle_dorsiflex_active` |

`expectedDominant` is a **set**, not a single id. Sit-to-stand is genuinely a hip, knee and trunk
movement and which one has the largest normalised range depends on the performer's proportions;
asserting one would make a correct recording look wrong.

### The exercise is chosen before recording, not inferred after it

The choice is what routes to the cyclic or the hold path, so it has to come first. Discovering that
the wall sit needed the hold flow only after eight reps have been refused is a worse way to find
out. The picker disables the wrong record button rather than letting the recording be refused a
minute later.

The choice also gives the bundle its §9.1 identity: `id`, `name`, `body_position` and the whole
`prescription`. Before this, every bundle was `rp_local` / "Recorded exercise" / 12 reps.

### Catalogue conformance is a warning, never a rejection

`checkAgainstCatalog` reports four disagreements — `VIEW`, `DOMINANT`, `COMPENSATION`, `MODE` — and
none of them stops anything.

This is deliberate. §3.8's validator rejects recordings that **cannot produce a usable reference**,
which is a property of the recording alone. These are recordings that are perfectly usable but
appear to be of something else, and only the person who pressed record can settle that. Two failures
it is there to catch:

- The physio picks the wrong entry, records a good set, and the bundle goes out under a name that
  does not describe it. §3.8 has nothing to say, because nothing is wrong with the recording.
- The physio records hip abduction side-on. The reference is fine; the patient will simply be
  calibrated to the wrong plane, and the app will be measuring depth on the one axis a single camera
  measures worst (§17.1).

`required_view` in the manifest still comes from **the view actually recorded**, not from the
catalogue. Overriding it would make calibration demand a view the reference was not built in, which
is a worse outcome than a name that disagrees with a plane.

`MODE` short-circuits: everything downstream of the mode is meaningless, so a view mismatch listed
underneath it would be noise.

`COMPENSATION` fires when a compensation joint is absent from the derived watch list. That absence
means the physio's own recording did not vary there, which is either a very clean demonstration or a
camera that cannot see the joint — and the physio is the only one who can tell those apart.

## Tests

`DemoExerciseCatalogTest`, 11 cases:

- the set is §3.3's four, in order, with unique ids;
- only the wall sit is a hold, and mode and `hold_seconds` agree — a counted exercise carrying a
  hold duration would produce a manifest that contradicts the path scoring it;
- abduction is the only frontal entry, and `manifestLabel()` spells it the way §9.1 does;
- every entry has a setup line, a safety line, an expected dominant and a compensation;
- a real authoring run over the synthetic knee recording matches seated knee extension;
- the same recording labelled hip abduction is flagged on both view and dominant;
- a recording that never settled on a view produces no `VIEW` warning — §3.8 check 6 already
  answered that and a second weaker voice on it helps nobody;
- an unwatched compensation is reported;
- a hold labelled cyclic, and a cyclic labelled hold, each produce exactly one `MODE` warning.

Command: `./gradlew testDebugUnitTest` — 528 tests, all pass. `./gradlew assembleDebug` builds.

## Outstanding

- **The device gate is unrun.** No exercise in this set has been recorded from a real body. The
  catalogue is a set of claims about four movements, and the only thing that tests them is a
  performer, a stand and a clear space. Until then this iteration is `in-progress`.
- **`faults.json` is still empty for all four.** §6.14's fault definitions are 8.1 and 8.2, and
  §6.15.1's masking table is unvalidated clinical logic pending C6. An exercise with no faults still
  scores, counts reps and drives the quality bar; it simply has nothing to say. Inventing
  placeholder faults here would put unvalidated clinical claims in front of a patient.
- **Hip abduction is the untested case in more than one sense.** It is the only frontal exercise, so
  it is the only one whose calibration view check has ever had to disagree with the default, and
  `hip_abduction_active` has never been the dominant feature of any recording the pipeline has seen.
- **The wall sit produces a `HoldReference`, not a bundle.** The hold path has no manifest and
  therefore no prescription, so `hold_seconds = 30` in the catalogue is currently read by nobody. It
  becomes live when 7.1 stores holds.
